# Financial Model

This model is deliberately simple. It is intended to test commercial plausibility before building a detailed accounting model.

## Core assumptions

Working assumptions for validation:

- Core managed service price: **$175/user/month**.
- Assured managed service price: **$225/user/month**.
- Average initial customer size: **25–35 users**.
- Paid diagnostic: **$2,500–$4,500**.
- Typical onboarding/remediation project: **$7,500–$20,000** depending on technical debt.
- Automation projects: **$7,500–$25,000**.
- Managed-service gross-margin design target after standardisation: **45–55%**.
- Microsoft and third-party licences are treated separately unless deliberately bundled.
- Early gross margins are expected to be lower because onboarding and engineering effort are disproportionately high.

These are commercial hypotheses, not guarantees.

## Unit economics example

A 30-user customer on SecureOps 365 at $175/user/month produces:

- Monthly recurring revenue: **$5,250**
- Annual recurring revenue: **$63,000**

At a 50% managed-service gross margin target, this would contribute approximately **$31,500 annual gross profit** before company overhead, sales, administration and taxes.

A 30-user SecureOps Assured customer at $225/user/month produces:

- Monthly recurring revenue: **$6,750**
- Annual recurring revenue: **$81,000**

At a 50% gross-margin design target, that equates to approximately **$40,500 annual gross profit** before company overhead.

## Scenario A — Lean founder-led launch

### Operating model

- Founder handles sales, solution design, QBR/vCIO and a substantial part of delivery.
- MDR/SOC outsourced.
- Field services subcontracted.
- Limited internal payroll.
- Avoid broad paid marketing.

### 12-month target

- 8 managed customers.
- Average 25 users/customer.
- 200 managed users.
- Weighted average managed price assumption: $190/user/month.

### Exit recurring run rate

200 users × $190 × 12 = **$456,000 ARR**.

Potential project revenue during the year may include diagnostics, onboarding and automation work, but it should not be counted as ARR.

### Why this scenario matters

This is the lowest-risk approach and is appropriate if the objective is to validate the model before hiring ahead of revenue.

## Scenario B — Balanced launch

### Operating model

- Founder leads commercial/architecture.
- One L1/L2 engineer added as demand proves out.
- Fractional service coordination/admin.
- Partner-led SOC/MDR and field delivery.
- Deliberate partner-referral strategy.

### 12-month target

- 15 managed customers.
- Average 30 users/customer.
- 450 managed users.
- Weighted average managed price: $195/user/month.

### Exit recurring run rate

450 × $195 × 12 = **$1,053,000 ARR**.

This is consistent with the earlier thesis that roughly 15–20 good customers can create a $1M+ recurring run rate without needing hundreds of accounts.

### Gross-profit design target

At a 45–55% gross margin, the managed-service component at this run rate would target approximately:

- 45%: **$473,850 gross profit**
- 50%: **$526,500 gross profit**
- 55%: **$579,150 gross profit**

before operating overhead.

## Scenario C — Aggressive growth launch

### Operating model

- Founder plus service engineer from early stage.
- Dedicated M365/security capability introduced sooner.
- Automation capability available early.
- Formal sales development and partner channel.
- Larger upfront operating cash requirement.

### 12-month target

- 24 managed customers.
- Average 35 users/customer.
- 840 managed users.
- Weighted average managed price: $200/user/month.

### Exit recurring run rate

840 × $200 × 12 = **$2,016,000 ARR**.

### Risk

This model is significantly more dangerous because staffing, vendor commitments and sales costs may be incurred before recurring revenue becomes predictable.

It should not be pursued unless early validation demonstrates a repeatable acquisition channel and strong close rates.

## Cash-flow warning

Exit ARR is not the same as revenue recognised during year one.

If customers are added progressively, first-year managed-service revenue may be much lower than the year-end ARR run rate.

Example: reaching $1M ARR in month 12 does not mean the company collected $1M of recurring service revenue during the preceding 12 months.

This distinction must be maintained in all planning.

## Customer concentration

Avoid reaching $1M ARR with one or two customers representing an excessive percentage of recurring revenue.

A working target should be that no single mature customer represents more than approximately 15% of total recurring revenue where practical.

## Pricing rules to test

1. Minimum monthly recurring commitment.
2. Mandatory paid onboarding/remediation.
3. Separate project pricing for non-standard environments.
4. Annual price review.
5. Clear limits around after-hours support.
6. Vendor and licensing pass-through with transparent margin policy.
7. Travel/field costs explicitly handled.
8. Automation support priced separately from standard IT support.

## What needs to be validated next

- Actual vendor costs for PSA, RMM, MDR/SOC, backup and documentation.
- Professional indemnity and cyber insurance costs.
- Average tickets per managed user.
- Engineer capacity per user under the chosen stack.
- Customer willingness to pay $175–$225/user/month.
- Average remediation cost before support begins.
- Sales cycle length by segment.
- Churn risk and contract term preference.
- Gross margin after field-service and partner costs.
- Cost of acquiring a customer through direct and partner channels.

## Financial decision rule

Do not scale headcount merely because prospects show interest.

Scale when there is evidence that:

- customers sign at target pricing;
- onboarding can be repeated;
- service load per customer is measurable;
- gross margins remain acceptable;
- referrals or outbound activity produce a repeatable pipeline.
