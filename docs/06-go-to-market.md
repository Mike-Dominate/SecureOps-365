# Go-to-Market Plan

## Objective

Validate whether Australian businesses in the target segments will pay for a combination of managed operations, cyber assurance and workflow improvement before scaling headcount or building proprietary software.

## Primary market motions

### Motion A — Professional services

Target:
- accounting firms;
- law firms;
- conveyancers;
- property businesses;
- engineering consultancies.

Typical triggers:
- AML/CTF programme implementation;
- cyber-insurance renewal;
- client security questionnaire;
- failed or untested backup;
- Microsoft 365 growth without governance;
- merger/acquisition;
- rapid hiring;
- data-handling concerns;
- desire to automate client onboarding or document workflows.

Message:

> Turn regulatory and client requirements into secure, repeatable technology workflows.

### Motion B — Mining/METS and field businesses

Target:
- engineering contractors;
- mining-service providers;
- drilling and maintenance firms;
- equipment suppliers;
- environmental and geological consultancies;
- field-service organisations.

Typical triggers:
- supplier security questionnaire;
- tender requirement;
- cyber-insurance renewal;
- new site or project;
- workforce growth;
- remote-access concerns;
- device sprawl;
- customer requirement to evidence controls.

Message:

> Win and retain contracts without scrambling to prove your IT security.

## Entry product

Do not lead with a generic free IT audit.

Lead with a short qualification conversation followed by a paid:

# Operations Risk & Waste Review

The review should give management three numbers or categories they can act on:

1. **Risk exposure** — where operations or security could materially fail.
2. **Technology waste** — avoidable licence, process or architecture cost.
3. **Automation opportunity** — recurring manual work worth removing.

The deliverable should be concise enough for an owner/CEO but technically defensible enough for follow-up remediation.

## First 100-account strategy

Build a manually researched list of approximately 100 target organisations rather than buying a broad SME database.

For each account, capture:

- organisation name;
- estimated employee count;
- industry;
- locations;
- field/regional footprint;
- apparent Microsoft/cloud signals where publicly available;
- recent growth or expansion;
- regulatory or customer-assurance pressure;
- likely operational pain;
- relevant executive or operations contact;
- personalised hypothesis for why a conversation is relevant.

The purpose is not to know their private environment before speaking with them. It is to avoid sending irrelevant generic MSP outreach.

## Outreach structure

### First contact

Use a specific trigger, not a feature list.

Examples:

- "How are you maintaining evidence for customer security questionnaires between tenders?"
- "Has your AML/CTF implementation changed the technology workflow for client onboarding?"
- "When a staff member leaves, how many systems must someone manually update?"
- "If your primary regional connection failed tomorrow, which business processes would stop?"

### Discovery conversation

Focus on:

- business impact;
- current ownership;
- repeated manual effort;
- risk and contractual pressure;
- existing provider gaps;
- willingness to standardise.

Do not begin by discussing product SKUs.

### Conversion

Qualified opportunity → paid review → remediation proposal → recurring managed service.

## Partner channel

### Compliance / AML specialists

They define the legal/compliance requirements; SecureOps implements technology workflows and evidence.

Value to partner:
- they retain advisory ownership;
- implementation is more likely to remain consistent after the initial project;
- customer receives operational evidence.

### Cyber-insurance brokers

Potential referral trigger:
- insurer questionnaire identifies missing controls;
- renewal premium is affected by security posture;
- customer cannot demonstrate MFA, backup, EDR or incident readiness.

### ISO / cyber / governance consultants

Partner where a customer needs formal framework advice or independent assurance beyond SecureOps' direct competence.

### Mining tender / procurement consultants

Referral trigger:
- customer receives a supplier-security questionnaire or tender requirement.

### OT cybersecurity specialists

SecureOps handles business IT and identity boundary; OT partner handles industrial environments.

### Regional field-service and connectivity partners

Use a controlled partner network for cabling, remote-site work, carrier services and onsite tasks outside practical travel range.

## Marketing assets to create

Do not begin with a large content programme.

Create a small number of assets closely tied to buying triggers:

1. "What evidence should you keep ready before a customer sends a cyber questionnaire?"
2. "Technology checklist for professional firms implementing AML/CTF workflows."
3. "The cost of manual joiner/leaver processes."
4. "Regional business continuity checklist: carrier, power, device and cloud failure."
5. "How to tell whether an AI automation actually saves money."

Each asset should lead to the paid diagnostic rather than a generic contact form.

## Sales qualification

Good-fit customers:
- 20–100 staff;
- meaningful dependence on Microsoft 365/cloud;
- owner/management willing to standardise;
- identifiable security/compliance/operational pain;
- willingness to pay for remediation;
- desire for an ongoing accountable provider.

Poor-fit customers:
- shopping solely for the cheapest helpdesk;
- unwilling to replace clearly unsafe legacy systems;
- expects unlimited project work inside a low monthly fee;
- refuses MFA or basic security controls;
- highly bespoke environment with no willingness to standardise;
- requires direct OT/SCADA responsibility initially.

## Validation metrics

Track from the first conversation:

- target accounts researched;
- conversations started;
- discovery calls held;
- paid reviews sold;
- review-to-remediation conversion;
- remediation-to-managed-service conversion;
- average managed users/customer;
- average recurring revenue/customer;
- sales-cycle length;
- acquisition source;
- gross margin;
- support load per user;
- automation project attach rate.

## Early validation thresholds

These are working decision thresholds, not industry benchmarks.

By the time 30–40 serious prospect conversations have occurred, the business should be able to answer:

- Do prospects understand the proposition without extensive education?
- Will they pay for the diagnostic?
- Will they pay target recurring pricing?
- Which trigger produces the shortest sales cycle?
- Which vertical generates the clearest repeated problem?
- Is partner-led acquisition materially more efficient than direct outbound?

If the answers remain unclear, refine the offer before expanding headcount.
