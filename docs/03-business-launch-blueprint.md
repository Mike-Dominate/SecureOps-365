# Business Launch Blueprint

## Business model

SecureOps 365 is designed as a **managed digital operations company**, not a traditional break/fix MSP.

The commercial model combines:

- paid diagnostics;
- implementation/remediation projects;
- per-user recurring managed services;
- specialist assurance add-ons;
- automation projects;
- recurring automation management;
- remote-site and resilience add-ons.

## Ideal customer profile

### Primary ICP

- 20–100 employees;
- Microsoft 365 already in use or a realistic target platform;
- no mature internal IT/security function;
- meaningful dependence on email, files, cloud applications and devices;
- identifiable regulatory, contractual, cyber-insurance or customer-assurance pressure;
- repetitive administration suitable for automation;
- willingness to standardise technology rather than preserve every legacy preference.

### Secondary ICP

- 100–150 employees where environment complexity remains compatible with the operating model;
- mining/METS and engineering contractors;
- multi-site or regional businesses;
- construction and field-service businesses;
- professional-services firms with sensitive client information.

## Launch focus

Initial market focus should be narrow enough to produce repeatability.

### Launch wedge A — Professional services

Target accounting, legal, conveyancing, property and engineering firms.

Problem statement:

> Regulatory and client requirements are increasing, but technology controls and workflows are inconsistent, manual and difficult to evidence.

Entry offer:

**Operations Risk & Waste Review**

Expansion path:

**SecureOps 365 → SecureOps Assured → RegulatedOps → Automation Sprint**

### Launch wedge B — Resources / field operations

Target mining/METS contractors, engineering firms and suppliers rather than Tier-1 operators initially.

Problem statement:

> Customers and insurers increasingly expect evidence of cyber controls, while distributed staff and sites create operational complexity.

Entry offer:

**ContractReady Resources Review**

Expansion path:

**SecureOps Assured → ContractReady Resources → FieldOps → Automation Sprint**

## Delivery principles

1. Standardise aggressively.
2. Use existing mature platforms rather than building infrastructure products internally.
3. Charge for onboarding and remediation.
4. Do not accept unsupported legacy complexity without explicit project pricing.
5. Measure outcomes, not ticket volume alone.
6. Maintain clear legal/compliance/OT boundaries.
7. Build repeatable evidence generation into normal operations.
8. Delay proprietary SaaS until repeated patterns are proven across customers.

## Customer lifecycle

**Discover → Baseline → Standardise → Secure → Automate → Operate → Measure → Improve**

### Discover

Understand:

- users;
- sites;
- business applications;
- network and connectivity;
- regulatory/contract obligations;
- critical processes;
- repeated manual work;
- current technology spend;
- business continuity dependencies.

### Baseline

Create an authoritative inventory of:

- identities;
- devices;
- licences;
- security configuration;
- backup;
- network assets;
- privileged accounts;
- external vendors;
- critical workflows.

### Standardise

Move the customer toward the approved architecture and reduce unnecessary variation.

### Secure

Implement the agreed baseline for identity, endpoint, email, backup, access, monitoring and incident readiness.

### Automate

Identify and remediate high-value repetitive work only where benefits can be reasonably demonstrated.

### Operate

Move into helpdesk, monitoring, administration, lifecycle management and partner coordination.

### Measure

Report on security posture, operational reliability, licence waste, support trends, automation outcomes and unresolved risks.

### Improve

Use quarterly reviews to identify the next material business improvement.

## Technology architecture — working standard

| Function | Preferred direction |
|---|---|
| Productivity | Microsoft 365 |
| Identity | Microsoft Entra ID |
| Endpoint management | Microsoft Intune |
| Endpoint protection | Microsoft Defender for Business |
| Email security | Microsoft Defender for Office 365 |
| Data governance | Microsoft Purview capabilities where justified |
| Automation | Power Automate / Logic Apps / Azure Functions |
| AI | Microsoft 365 Copilot / Copilot Studio / Azure AI where justified |
| SIEM | Microsoft Sentinel for appropriate larger customers |
| PSA | Mature commercial PSA platform |
| RMM | Mature commercial RMM platform |
| Documentation | Dedicated MSP documentation platform |
| Backup | Standardised third-party platform(s) |
| MDR/SOC | Partner-delivered initially |
| Networking | Small approved vendor set |
| Regional connectivity | Primary + secondary carrier/4G/5G/LEO patterns as appropriate |
| Reporting | Power BI initially; proprietary dashboard only later |

Vendor selections should be tested commercially before contracts are signed.

## Staffing model

### Founder-led stage

Founder handles:

- sales;
- architecture;
- customer discovery;
- vCIO/QBR activity;
- service design;
- partner relationships.

Use partners for:

- MDR/SOC;
- OT cybersecurity;
- specialist compliance/legal advice;
- field work outside practical travel range.

### At approximately 100–250 managed users

Add:

- L1/L2 service engineer;
- part-time service coordination/admin as workload requires.

### At approximately 250–500 managed users

Add:

- dedicated M365/security engineer;
- automation/cloud capability;
- stronger service coordination.

### Beyond approximately 500 users

Evaluate:

- service desk lead;
- account/vCIO capacity;
- dedicated automation resource;
- sales development capacity;
- regional field network expansion.

Do not build an internal 24x7 SOC at this stage.

## Business boundaries

### In scope

- business IT;
- Microsoft 365;
- identity;
- endpoints;
- business networking;
- backup/recovery;
- business cyber controls;
- workflow automation;
- AI governance implementation;
- evidence generation;
- supplier/customer security responses with customer approval;
- regional connectivity coordination.

### Out of scope initially

- safety-critical OT/SCADA/PLC engineering;
- legal advice;
- formal assurance claims beyond actual assessor competence;
- bespoke support for arbitrary legacy platforms without project approval;
- unsupported consumer technology estates;
- custom software products with no validated repeatability.

## Long-term product direction

The future platform should only be built after recurring customer evidence shows the same problems repeatedly.

Potential future platform functions:

- identity posture;
- device compliance;
- backup/recovery evidence;
- licence optimisation;
- supplier-assurance packs;
- cyber-insurance evidence;
- workflow automation metrics;
- AI governance status;
- open risk tracking;
- quarterly improvement roadmap.

The objective is to let the service business discover the software product rather than guessing it in advance.
