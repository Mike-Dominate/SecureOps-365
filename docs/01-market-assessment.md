# Australian IT Market Assessment

## Executive thesis

Australian SMEs do not simply need more software. Many need help making their existing technology secure, integrated, measurable and easier to operate.

The opportunity identified is a productised managed digital-operations model rather than a generic break/fix MSP. The working proposition is:

> Operate the workplace, prove the controls, and continuously remove avoidable manual work.

## Core market problems identified

1. Skills shortages and limited internal IT capability.
2. Rising cyber risk and greater pressure to evidence controls.
3. Poorly managed Microsoft 365, identity, devices and backup environments.
4. Repetitive manual administration across onboarding, approvals, reporting and document handling.
5. AI experimentation without consistent governance or measurable outcomes.
6. SaaS and cloud spend that grows without clear ownership or optimisation.
7. Regional and field-based organisations needing resilient connectivity and remote support.
8. Mining/METS contractors facing customer, insurer and supply-chain cyber requirements.

## Attractive customer profile

The current sweet spot is approximately **20–100 employees**, extending toward 150 where operational complexity remains manageable.

These organisations are large enough to experience meaningful identity, endpoint, cyber, workflow and compliance problems but often too small to justify a complete internal team spanning CIO, security, infrastructure, helpdesk and automation roles.

## Priority verticals

### Professional services

Accounting, legal, conveyancing, property, engineering and related firms are attractive because they:

- rely heavily on Microsoft 365 and document-centric workflows;
- hold sensitive information;
- face regulatory and client-assurance obligations;
- have high-value knowledge workers whose manual time is expensive;
- are realistic candidates for AI and workflow automation.

### Mining/METS contractors and suppliers

The better initial resources-market wedge is not Tier-1 mine operators. It is the ecosystem of contractors, engineering companies, drilling firms, equipment suppliers, environmental consultancies, transport providers and other METS organisations.

The service opportunity is around business IT, identity, endpoint security, cyber evidence, supplier questionnaires, backup, workforce lifecycle and remote workforce support.

**Initial boundary:** safety-critical OT, SCADA, PLC and industrial control work should be delivered only through appropriately qualified specialist partners.

### Construction and field services

This market combines a large SME population with recurring problems around:

- mobile workers;
- quoting;
- job documentation;
- photos and evidence;
- timesheets;
- supplier communication;
- onboarding;
- approvals;
- invoicing;
- device and identity management.

### Regional and distributed businesses

Regionality is not treated as a standalone industry. It changes the operating architecture.

Regional customers may require:

- dual connectivity;
- cellular or LEO satellite backup;
- resilient site networking;
- remote device management;
- standardised replacement hardware;
- local field-service partners;
- cloud-first recovery plans.

### Healthcare and regulated SMEs

Potentially strong demand, especially for cyber and privacy, but this should be entered after the service operating model is mature because consequences of failure and compliance complexity are higher.

## Market conclusion

The strongest current business concept is:

**Managed Digital Operations for Australian SMEs**

with four linked capabilities:

- **Operate** — users, devices, Microsoft 365, cloud, networking and support.
- **Protect** — cyber controls, monitoring, backup and recovery.
- **Assure** — evidence for insurers, customers, contracts and regulatory workflows.
- **Improve** — automation, AI and measurable process improvement.

## Strategic progression

Do not build a large SaaS platform before validating repeated customer problems.

Preferred progression:

**Specialised consulting → Productised services → Recurring managed operations → Automation IP → Proprietary platform**

## Evidence base used in the research

The market work used current Australian sources including:

- Australian Bureau of Statistics business counts and Characteristics of Australian Business data;
- Australian Signals Directorate cyber threat and Essential Eight guidance;
- OAIC Notifiable Data Breaches reporting;
- AUSTRAC material on expanded AML/CTF obligations;
- Department of Industry resources and energy data;
- Jobs and Skills Australia regional labour-market material;
- Infrastructure Australia / Department of Infrastructure regional telecommunications material;
- current Australian MSP, cyber, automation and mining-IT competitor offerings.

Specific claims should be refreshed before use in external sales materials because market data, prices and regulatory guidance change over time.
