# 90-Day Execution Plan

## Goal

Move SecureOps 365 from researched concept to evidence-backed commercial validation without prematurely hiring, overbuilding the stack or creating proprietary software.

## Days 1–30 — Define and prepare

### Commercial
- Finalise Operations Risk & Waste Review scope.
- Finalise SecureOps 365 and SecureOps Assured inclusions/exclusions.
- Finalise RegulatedOps and ContractReady boundaries.
- Draft proposal, statement of work and onboarding assumptions.
- Define minimum monthly recurring commitment and onboarding rules.

### Technical
- Shortlist PSA, RMM, documentation, backup and MDR/SOC platforms.
- Define the Microsoft baseline: Entra, Intune, Defender, Business Premium assumptions.
- Create a standard device / identity / backup / security checklist.
- Define minimum customer acceptance standards.
- Define what is explicitly unsupported.

### Partnerships
- Identify at least 3 AML/compliance specialists or legal advisers for referral discussions.
- Identify at least 2 cyber-insurance brokers.
- Identify at least 2 OT cyber specialists.
- Identify regional field-service/connectivity partners.

### Sales preparation
- Build first 50 professional-services targets.
- Build first 25 mining/METS/engineering targets.
- Build first 25 construction/field-service targets.
- Create one-page diagnostic overview.
- Create simple discovery script.

### Exit criteria for Day 30
- Service scope is documented.
- First 100-account target universe exists.
- At least 5 partner conversations have occurred.
- Vendor shortlist and estimated per-user cost stack exist.
- Diagnostic is ready to sell.

---

## Days 31–60 — Validate willingness to pay

### Market activity
- Conduct targeted founder-led outreach.
- Hold 15–20 serious discovery conversations.
- Sell first paid Operations Risk & Waste Reviews.
- Capture objections verbatim.
- Record which trigger caused each conversation.

### Delivery validation
- Perform first reviews using the same template.
- Measure actual hours required.
- Identify where information is difficult to obtain.
- Record remediation patterns that repeat across customers.

### Commercial validation
- Present remediation and recurring-service proposals.
- Test target $175–$225/user pricing.
- Test minimum monthly commitment.
- Test customer reaction to mandatory paid onboarding/remediation.

### Exit criteria for Day 60
Preferred evidence:
- 15+ meaningful discovery conversations;
- 3+ paid reviews;
- at least 1 recurring-service proposal accepted or in final-stage negotiation;
- actual vendor cost estimates available;
- first version of onboarding runbook updated from real delivery.

If paid reviews cannot be sold, pause scaling and revisit positioning before hiring.

---

## Days 61–90 — Convert and standardise

### Customer acquisition
- Convert strongest reviews into SecureOps customers.
- Target 2–4 initial managed customers.
- Prioritise customers willing to standardise and participate in case-study measurement.

### Operations
- Document the real onboarding sequence.
- Build reusable remediation checklists.
- Standardise reporting and evidence collection.
- Establish escalation paths with MDR/SOC and specialist partners.

### Automation
- Identify at least one measurable workflow problem from each initial customer.
- Do not automate merely to demonstrate AI.
- Select workflows with clear baseline time/cost and manageable operational risk.

### Measurement
For each customer establish baseline metrics:
- managed users;
- managed devices;
- MFA coverage;
- patch/compliance status;
- backup/recovery status;
- licence waste;
- unresolved risk items;
- support volume;
- manual process hours targeted for reduction.

### Exit criteria for Day 90
Target position:
- 2–4 recurring customers;
- documented onboarding process;
- known per-user vendor/tool cost;
- known early support load;
- first case study or measurable improvement story;
- evidence on which vertical/message converts best;
- decision on whether to remain lean or hire first service engineer.

# Decision gates

## Gate 1 — After 20 serious prospect conversations

Ask:
- Is the pain real?
- Is the buyer identifiable?
- Is the proposition understood?
- Are customers willing to pay for diagnosis?

If no, refine the wedge.

## Gate 2 — After 3 paid reviews

Ask:
- Are review findings repeatable?
- Can the review be delivered profitably?
- Is there a natural remediation path?
- Does recurring management logically follow?

If no, redesign the diagnostic.

## Gate 3 — After 3 managed customers

Ask:
- What is the real support load?
- Which vendor costs were underestimated?
- Which environments should be rejected?
- Which tasks are repeatedly manual internally?
- Is gross margin trending toward the design target?

Only then commit to meaningful hiring.

## Gate 4 — After 10–20 customers

Ask:
- Which customer problems repeat across the estate?
- Which evidence must be collected repeatedly?
- Which workflows are being rebuilt again and again?
- Which reporting would customers pay to see continuously?

Only at this point should a proprietary platform be seriously considered.
